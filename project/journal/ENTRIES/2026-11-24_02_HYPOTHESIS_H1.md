# HYPOTHESIS — 2026-11-24 — written before any experiment
Problem as I see it: flow-based intrusion detectors are evaluated on random splits of a single capture and score ~99 %, but in practice they are installed on a different network. I believe that when a detector trained on three NetFlow-V3 networks is tested on the fourth, its macro-F1 will collapse, and that much of the collapse comes from "environment signature" features — features whose benign-traffic distributions differ between networks (TTL, TCP window size, DNS/FTP fields, destination port). The model learns "this network's benign looks like X" instead of "attacks look like Y".

My first idea for a fix (to be tested on validation only):
(1) ESF — drop features whose benign distributions let a simple classifier tell the training networks apart;
(2) PEN — re-express every feature relative to its own network's distribution (per-network quantile normalization using unlabeled traffic), so absolute offsets between networks disappear.
I expect ESF+PEN to raise leave-one-network-out macro-F1 over tuned LightGBM, with ≤1 point loss in-distribution. I am unsure PEN will survive BoT-IoT, where ~99.7 % of flows are attacks, so "the network's distribution" is mostly attack traffic.
